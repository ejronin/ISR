What this is: This atlas consolidates publicly verifiable records of the 2026 Iran conflict into one auditable operational picture. It is not built to validate a political, religious or national narrative. Claims are retained when they are uncomfortable, rejected when they fail verification, and revised when better evidence arrives. Comfort is not an evidentiary category.

Evidence doctrine

  • Observation is not attribution.
  • Actor claim is not confirmation.
  • Damage is not incapacity.
  • A weapon launch is not proof of impact.
  • Unknown is not shown as zero.

Source doctrine

Primary releases establish what an actor officially said or did. Reuters, AP, AFP and comparable factual-spine sources are used for cross-checking. Satellite and technical BDA are preferred for physical effects. State media establish claim provenance; they do not self-verify battle damage.

Geographic precision

Precision follows evidence. Exact fixed sites are mapped only when publicly supported. City, province or sector-level evidence stays coarse. Schematic logistics routes are not live AIS tracks, and the atlas does not publish targeting-quality locations for current mobile military assets.

Damage vs operational effect

The board separates what was physically damaged from what function was lost and whether that effect persisted. A destroyed radar can be a serious local loss without proving that the parent base or regional air-defense network became inoperable.

Functional-damage scale used here
Decisive local loss = the named asset/node was destroyed or permanently lost. Major subfacility loss = a specific command, radar, air-defense or support function became inoperable or had to relocate. Significant/moderate damage = real degradation with redundancy or continued site operation. Unverified = an actor said it happened but independent evidence is insufficient. These are functional assessments, not political judgments.
